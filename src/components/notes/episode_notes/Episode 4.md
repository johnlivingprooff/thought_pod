Have you ever experienced the feeling of nothingness this vast vague emptiness that doesn't have direction or meaning and it feels deeper than being lost, but you can't feel anything so you wonder what is there to feel 

I think it's worse than depression because at least with depression you feel a heaviness and a sense of deep sorrow and and the difficulty to do a lot of things but with nothingness you can still do so many things because you don't even know what you feel like. 

And in a very weird and manipulative way, it dictates your affairs, because it's like I don't understand what I feel so I just called it  nothing and shoved it away, ignoring the emptiness inside, and every time it bothers me beyond what I can ignore, I look for distractions, to tune out the loudness of this nothing. 

If you resonate with this, I think I've discovered why it's like this, now I don't know why you ignore this emptiness or feel for nothing, but when I decided to embrace this vast vague emptiness, I realised my soul felt empty because it had a longing, I had no understanding to satisfy. 

I realised, it wasn't really emptiness, it was a desire, a deep longing, a space that seemed to grow infinitely. 

So how do you satisfy an infinite desire? To answer this we'll go back to the beginning to understand the condition of man. 

In Genesis 2, from verse 7:
God forms man from the dust, then breathes into his nostrils, and man becomes a living soul

What's interesting, and I think, important to note is that breathing is desired by the body to sustain life, and the desire for air isn't just satisfied with one breath, it's the continuous exercise that sustains life. 

I've come to understand that we all live with this innate desire to remain connected with the source of life. 

So, confronting this emptiness made me realise, this isn't something I can ignore or run from, it's a deep call to reconnect with the only being of infinite satisfaction that can occupy this expanding desire for life. 

The more I wanted to live, the more I needed to breathe, but

I had allowed the condition of ignorance, to keep me from the beauty that this empty space allowed me to see about me. The emptiness I had ignored was a calling to return. 

Maybe that's why ignoring the feeling of emptiness, keeps us suffocating. 

And this is why Christ came, so that we can learn to breathe again.
