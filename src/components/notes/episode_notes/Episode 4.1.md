*The Flesh
**Core Framework** - Condition

I remember asking myself once - "What does Paul mean when he says to kill the flesh?" - And thinking about the violence that goes with the word kill seems to make this proposition very serious and gruesome. 

Because, the idea to kill something would stem from a hatred of whatever is about to get a cruel termination, or this thing is a dangerous threat that needs to be permanently eliminated

So I discovered, reading Romans 6:6, that our old-self is crucified with Christ, and Paul says that this is so that the sinful body might be done away with, and that we no longer serve sin.

So what is this "flesh"?

Paul describes it as the body of sin, so this must mean that it could be likened to a container for the nature of sin. So the flesh is this old rusty thing that has been corrupted by what it holds/contains. The content of flesh has been corrupted by sin.

It's the idea that what used to be clean and presentable has allowed something wicked to corrupt it's way of life.

I've been on this thought for a while, especially around the question of content, you know, what is fresh? is it just the re-imaging of something stale till it becomes relevant-and I think it's important to talk about this, because whatever is repeated over time to an audience has the potential to quickly gain relevance, and influence; so if something stale is constantly being re-imaged as fresh, the audience will soon start to accept stale as the new fresh, and this ploy to keep people in corruption will progressively become successful

And this is why I think it's necessary to give proper definitions to concepts - "fresh" is not a twisted version of something that is old and stale. But an actual rejuvenation of what was old and not twisting it and keeping it in the same state of staleness, but giving it a completely new makeover.

So like Jesus said in Matthew and in all the other Gospels, you can't put new wine into old wineskins because new wine still has the potential to expand and ferment and grow, but an old wineskin has been completely stretched and will burst if new wine is poured into it. As the new wine begins to expand, the old skin cannot continue to expand because it's completely expanded, it's old, so it bursts and wastes the new wine. 

So when Paul talks about killing the flesh or says that we die to the flesh when we are crucified with Christ. He talks about an abandonment of the old container of sin because the container is corrupt and has been fully expanded by what is evil and what is wicked. Something new, fresh, and clean cannot be put into something that's old, corrupt, and wicked.

This is exactly why we need to get rid of the container of sin - the flesh that has been corrupted by sin has to be completely destroyed. In the same way that a bed bug-infested bed or bed sheet or mattress will be completely burnt with fire, so also a person who used to walk in the way of the flesh they used to walk carnally will have to go through a complete refinement and change, a new makeover, so that there is truly freshness and it's not just "oh I still have my old ways" - "I still but I'm just pretending to be a new person". But it's a completely new transformation that happens.
