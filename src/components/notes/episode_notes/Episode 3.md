## **Unpacking Sin, Guilt, and Shame:**
Reflective Questions to explore:

- *`“Why does guilt feel different from shame? And how does shame affect how I see myself?”`*

	- Use this to discuss the transition from guilt (something you’ve done) to shame (who you think you are).

***"What I've done is sinful, it makes me feel guilty; the alarm telling me something is wrong with what I've done. Guilt then transitions to shame—So, what is shame?"***

***"This is what I think shame is—A deep emotional response that arises from my believe that something is wrong with not just what I've done, but who I am. It's a distortion on how I perceive myself based on my actions"***

***"Unlike guilt, which is “I did something bad,” shame is “I am bad.”  Shame affects my identity, distorting it, because it's an internalized belief that my flaws, and failures make me unworthy of connection, acceptance, LOVE"***

***"Most times, shames causes us to hide away, like Adam did in v10: he said, I heard your voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself."***

## **The Distortion of Identity**

- ***`“What happens when we stop seeing ourselves clearly? What happens when we start seeing ourselves through the lens of shame?”`***
    
    - Introduce how shame distorts self-perception and makes people forget their original identity as reflectors of God’s image—tie this with Adam's hiding away because he realizes that he's naked.

	- Talk about the broken relationship and how we hide from God, referencing Genesis 3:8-10 (Adam and Eve hiding after they sinned).

	- "v10: And he said, I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself."

***"Someone that would be with God in the cool of the evening, and enjoyed the presence of God, now feels disconnected not just because of the act of disobedience, but because his action makes him ashamed of being associated with the sacredness of God's presence"***

***"The dynamic of Sin, Guilt & Shame, somehow shapes the nature or identity of us Humans. It is difficult for us to separate ourselves from what we've done, possibly because we are still in hiding, in the dark, afraid of the light and what it can expose in us!"***

***"So, what happens when we stop seeing ourselves clearly? We hide away in shame, rejecting light that we are created to reflect. The feeling of disconnect between God and ourselves deepens, we become lost."***

***"We start believing the lies that shame places on us; moving from a belief that I've done something wrong, to maybe something is wrong with me, maybe I'm the horrible person; falling into a place of self-depreciation"***

## **Distorted View of God**

- ***`“When I’m stuck in shame, how does it change how I see God?”`***
    
    - Explore how shame distorts our view of God: instead of a loving Father, He becomes an angry judge or distant observer.

***“Now that I see myself as a sinful, depraved human, it naturally affects how I view God. Instead of seeing Him for who He truly is—the Light that exposes and disperses the darkness I’ve embraced—I begin to misinterpret His purifying process. What is meant to be a judgment of my actions, I start to receive as condemnation of my identity; it becomes God is condemning me”***

## **The Disconnect Between Us and God**

- ***`“How does shame separate us from God, even though He’s always been there?”`***
    
***“I begin to see God as nothing more than an angry judge, missing the love wrapped in His chastisement. Or maybe worse—I see Him as distant and unconcerned, a silent observer with no interest in being involved with me. And so, I sink deeper into this facade of disconnect, not realizing He’s always been present—waiting. Waiting for me to recognize and embrace His light, and return to the original purpose of my existence.”***

## **Moment of Pause**

- _“Maybe what’s broken in us has broken our view of God too. Maybe it’s not just about hiding from Him... maybe we’ve stopped recognizing Him altogether.”_

- *“But what if God never stopped seeing the real me? What if there’s still a way to be restored—to reflect Him again?”*

**"You see, Hebrews 12:2 talks about Jesus—the author and finisher of our faith—who, for the joy set before Him, endured the cross, despising the shame, and is now seated at the right hand of the throne of God."**

**"The significance of this verse is profound. Jesus took on the sin of the world—from that first act of disobedience in the garden, to every disobedience we still display today. He felt the full weight of guilt, yet He rejected the shame. He despised it. He endured the pain of the cross, but overcame the shame it carried—because there was a greater purpose. He became the source of our salvation—offering redemption to anyone who believes in His ultimate sacrifice. A sacrifice made to free us from the empty pursuit of worldly desires that pull us away from our true purpose: to be a living expression of the One we were made to reflect and worship."**
